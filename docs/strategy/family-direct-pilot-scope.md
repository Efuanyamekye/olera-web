# Family-direct pilot: what it takes to launch by mid-October

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Follows:** `not-an-agency-caregiver-marketplace.md` (the memo, sections 6 to 8)
**Question:** can a minimal family-direct pilot be live by mid-October, so the January application carries traction and not just a plan?

## The short answer

Yes, but only as a **hand-run concierge pilot in Dallas-Fort Worth with paid demand behind it**. Olera's own family traffic is too thin in any single metro to produce traction by January. The densest metro sends about two home-care families a month. And the launch date depends on one thing Olera doesn't control: how fast counsel signs off on the model.

Nothing needs building to start. The first ten families are matched by a person, the way the first ad leads were.

## 1. Demand: thinner than the 225 posts suggest

Verified read-only against the live database on 1 Oct 2026.

| Measure | Count |
|---|---|
| Active published care posts | 228, spread across about 200 cities; no city has more than 5 |
| Families who sent an inquiry, last 90 days | 807 |
| ...of those tagged "Home Care" (non-medical, the pilot's scope) | 132, about 1 in 6 |

Inquiring families in the last 90 days, by metro (matched on profile city):

| Metro | Families | Tagged Home Care |
|---|---|---|
| Dallas-Fort Worth | 27 | 5 |
| St. Louis | 23 | 2 |
| Atlanta | 22 | 6 |
| Los Angeles | 22 | 2 |
| Kansas City | 18 | 0 |
| Long Island | 13 | 0 |

So the best metro yields about **two home-care families a month** organically. A pilot judged on "half of requests matched within 14 days" needs more requests than that to say anything by December. The pilot has to buy its demand.

Two cautions on these numbers:
- Care type is whatever the family ticked, and many families looking at independent or assisted living would also take help at home. The real pool is larger than 132, but it hasn't been measured.
- Profile city is where the family said they are, which isn't always where care is needed.

## 2. The metro: Dallas-Fort Worth

- **Most demand** of any metro (27 inquiring families in 90 days).
- **Texas law is already researched.** Chantel's 3 Sep competitor research works through Texas Health and Safety Code § 142.003, the exemptions Care.com, CareLinx, CareYaya and Papa rely on. No other state has that head start.
- **Ad infrastructure exists.** The September city-ads experiment built a DFW arm. Whether it's still running needs checking before any spend.
- **No paying provider there today.** Assisting Hands Dallas (Robbie) wants North Texas preferred-provider status but isn't paying. TJ has said competing with customers isn't a concern; it's flagged so the Robbie conversation isn't a surprise.

## 3. What it needs, in the order it blocks

| # | Item | Owner | Blocks launch? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Counsel confirms the registry model in Texas**, in writing: companion and homemaker scope, family as employer, and whether Olera may charge a fee without losing the § 142.003(a)(3) registry exemption | TJ engages counsel | **Yes** | The one item whose timing nobody at Olera controls. Chantel's notes: keeping client records, directing services or paying the caregiver voids the registry exemption. A family fee may or may not; counsel decides. |
| 2 | **Insurance**: general liability plus professional liability (E&O) for a referral service | TJ, through a broker | Yes | No quote yet. Texas lets private employers opt out of workers' comp, which may simplify things for families as employers; counsel to confirm. |
| 3 | **Vetting standard and vendor**: background check, Texas Employee Misconduct Registry lookup, two references, video interview | TJ picks the vendor | Yes, for the first match | Vendor cost not quoted yet. The registry lookup is believed to be a free public search; verify. |
| 4 | **Caregiver recruiting**: Indeed posts and Facebook groups in DFW, with MedJobs students as one source among several | Ops owner | No; runs in parallel from day one | Stop line: fewer than 15 vetted after 4 weeks. |
| 5 | **Family demand**: the DFW ad arm pointed at a family-direct page or instant form, plus the DFW families already on Olera | TJ approves spend | No; starts once 5 caregivers are vetted | At the September cost per lead ($76 target, $151 measured on 18 Sep), 20 requests cost about $1,500 to $3,000. |
| 6 | **Agreements**: a family terms page (family is the employer, Olera vets and introduces) and a caregiver terms page | Counsel drafts | Yes | Comes out of item 1. |
| 7 | **Ops owner**: one person who calls families, matches, follows up at day 7 and day 30 | TJ decides | Yes | Provider calls are TJ and Ces only. Families can follow the same rule. |
| 8 | **Tooling** | Nobody yet | No | A shared sheet and the existing inbox. Build only after ten matches show what repeats. |

## 4. Timeline, if counsel answers in two weeks

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 to 14 Oct | Engage counsel; get insurance and vetting quotes; post caregiver jobs (recruiting needs no sign-off) |
| ~15 Oct | Counsel confirms. First caregivers vetted. **Launch.** |
| 15 Oct to 15 Nov | Ads on; first families matched by hand |
| 15 Nov | Supply stop line: 15 vetted caregivers or stop |
| 15 Nov to 20 Dec | Matches continue; 30-day rebooking readings start arriving |
| ~20 Dec | Numbers frozen for the application |

If counsel takes six weeks instead of two, launch moves to mid-November. January then shows about a month of matching and almost no rebooking data, which is the point where **April becomes the honest date.**

## 5. What January could show, if it works

The stop lines are the floor. At 20 to 30 paid family requests and the proposed bars:
- 15 or more vetted caregivers;
- 10 to 15 families matched;
- a first rebooking rate from the families matched before about 20 November;
- revenue only if counsel allows a fee and TJ chooses to charge one.

Whether a reviewer reads that as commercial traction depends mostly on the last line. Matches without money are evidence of demand and supply, not of a business.

## 6. Decisions only TJ can make

1. **Engage counsel this week.** Nothing else unlocks the date.
2. **Charge families or not.** A match fee is what turns matches into commercial traction. It is also the thing counsel has to clear.
3. **Approve the ad spend**, about $1,500 to $3,000 at September cost per lead.
4. **Name the ops owner.**

If counsel can't answer by about 1 November, submit in April.

## What this scope leaves alone

MedJobs and Managed Ads keep running. No CRP document changes until the pilot has numbers. Nothing is built before ten matches.
