# P5 · Partner Recruitment

> **Owner:** Sess
> **Goal:** university stakeholders who put the program in front of students, repeatedly.
> **Runs in parallel with the provider funnel from week one — never after it.**
> **Companion spec:** `../../PARTNER_RECRUITMENT_SYSTEM.md` (engineering).

---

## 1. Why this protocol decides whether MedJobs works

The build plan states the constraint plainly: *"throughput is ultimately gated by student supply."* We can
sign providers faster than we can staff them. A Client with no candidates is a Client who churns — and it is
the hardest-won relationship we have.

A Partner is **not a customer and not an employer.** They are a trusted intermediary who shares the program
with students. The ask is small on purpose: *help us tell your students about this.*

---

## 2. Who to target — in this order

| Priority | Subtype | Why | The ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pre-health advisor** | Talks to exactly our students, all day, and is measured on their outcomes | Forward to the pre-health list; mention it in advising |
| **2** | **Student org leader** (pre-med, pre-nursing, AMSA, public health) | Owns the GroupMe/Discord/Slack where students actually are | Post the flyer; mention it at a meeting |
| **3** | **Department head** (nursing, public health, kinesiology) | Unlocks professors and formal channels | Permission to reach professors; share with the department |
| 4 | Professor | Highest-trust channel — a class visit converts | **Gated:** only after a department head grants permission |
| — | Career center | Broad but unfocused | Backup channel |

**Target 15–25 named humans per site.** Named, with an email and ideally a phone number — not a
`preprofessional@university.edu` inbox.

**E-5 will tell us which subtype actually activates fastest.** Until then this order is a hypothesis based on
proximity to the student, not a finding.

---

## 3. Building the list

For each site:

1. University pre-health / pre-med advising pages → advisor names.
2. Student organization directory → current officers. **Check the year** — org rosters go stale every May,
   and a message to last year's president goes nowhere.
3. Department pages → department heads for nursing, public health, kinesiology, biology.
4. Career services → the health-careers contact.

Capture: name, title, email, phone, department, and **why they are the right person**. That last field is
what makes the outreach personal instead of templated.

Create them as Partner Prospects in the **Partners** tab with the correct subtype — the subtype drives the
email tone automatically (formal for department heads, peer-to-peer for org leaders).

---

## 4. Outreach

Same machinery as providers: pre-flight → cadence → replies → meeting → activation. Subtype-aware copy
already exists.

### What to say

**Advisor:**
> *"I'm a physician working with Olera. We run a student caregiver program that places pre-health students in
> paid caregiving roles with local home care agencies — real patient hours, paid, plus a recommendation. I'd
> love 15 minutes to show you, and if it's useful for your students, to ask whether you'd share it."*

**Student org leader:**
> *"We connect pre-health students with paid caregiving jobs near campus — real patient hours that count, and
> a recommendation at the end. Would your members find this useful? Happy to send a flyer you can post."*

**Department head:**
> *"I'm writing to ask permission to share a student caregiving program with faculty in your department, and
> to introduce it to you first."*

### The ask is small and specific

Not "will you partner with us." **"Will you send this flyer to your pre-health list?"** or **"Can I post this
in your group chat?"** Small asks get yes. Partnership language triggers a review process.

---

## 5. Activation — what makes a Partner real

A Partner is activated when they have **agreed to distribute** and you have logged **distribution evidence**:

| Evidence type | Means |
|---|---|
| Verbal commitment | They said yes on a call |
| Email forward | They forwarded it to their list |
| Flyer share | Posted to a group chat, board, or social |
| Class visit | They will let us present |

**Log the evidence on the row.** Add, as a note: **how many students it plausibly reached.** Rough is fine —
"~200 on the pre-health list" is infinitely more useful than nothing, and it is currently the only way to
tell a good partner from a nominal one (see the attribution gap in `../03-METRICS.md §7`).

---

## 6. Keeping partners warm

A one-time flyer share produces a one-time trickle. The value is in repetition.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2 weeks after distribution | *"Three students from \[University\] have applied — thank you."* Feedback makes them share again |
| Monthly | A short note: how many students, any placements |
| **When a student gets hired** | **Tell them. Immediately.** This is the single most effective retention message we have |
| Semester start | Refresh the ask — new students, new officers |
| Annually | Re-check org leadership. Rosters turn over every spring |

**Ask every activated partner for one colleague.** Referrals inside a university are how three partners
becomes twelve, and it is a far cheaper channel than cold outreach.

| Day | What |

## 8. Escalate

| Situation | To |
|---|---|
| A partner wants to talk to a physician | **Logan.** This is exactly what he is for — *"put me in front of advisors, student orgs, students"* |
| A university asks for a formal agreement or MOU | Logan |
| A department head refuses professor access | Log it; work the other subtypes |
| Distribution happening but no applications arriving | Sess → the flyer is the problem (**E-4**), not the partner |
