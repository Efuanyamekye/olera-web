# P6 · Student Supply

> **Owner:** Sess
> **Goal:** turn flyer impressions into **live, vetted candidates** a Client would actually interview.
> **The measure that matters:** live candidates per active Client (**H4**), target ≥ 5:1.

---

## 1. The pipeline

```
Flyer / QR code / link
      ↓
Application started
      ↓  ← the biggest drop, and the most recoverable
Application completed
      ↓
Vetted + activated
      ↓
LIVE CANDIDATE  →  invited to interview  →  hired
```

**Every stage is human-shepherded in 2.0.** Logan is explicit that we should not skip this: *"It would be
amazing if this whole system ran on its own… You don't have to get there yet."* Students who are hand-held
through the application complete it; students who are left alone do not.

---

## 2. Distribution — getting the flyer in front of students

The flyer, with its QR code, is the primary acquisition asset. Channels, best first:

| Channel | Via | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-health advisor email list | Partner | Highest quality — a trusted sender to the exact audience |
| Student org group chat (GroupMe/Discord/Slack) | Partner | Highest volume, fastest response |
| Org meeting mention | Partner | Highest conversion — social proof in the room |
| Class announcement | Professor (permissioned) | Highest trust, slowest to arrange |
| Physical posting — student union, health sciences building | Anyone | Cheap; keep doing it |
| Instagram — org accounts | Partner | Good for reach, weak for intent |

> ⚠️ **The flyer is a known weak asset.** Logan: *"It's a terrible flyer, but it's a functional flyer."*
> Diana's version went through three iterations and worked. Recover it (**O-6**) or redesign it (**E-4**).
> This is the single cheapest lever on supply volume.

**What the flyer must say — three things, in this order:**

1. **Paid** caregiving work near campus
2. **Real patient-contact hours** toward your application
3. **A recommendation** when you finish

Plus: who it is for (pre-health students), the time commitment, and a QR code straight to the application.

## 3. Shepherding applications

Check daily. **A stalled application is recoverable for about 48 hours and then it is gone.**

| State | Action | When |
|---|---|---|
| Started, not finished | **Email or text: one sentence, offer help** | Within 24h |
| Finished, unverified | Review and verify | Within 24h |
| Verified | Activate → live candidate | Same day |
| Stalled >48h after a nudge | One more nudge, then leave it | Day 3 |

**The nudge that works** is short, human, and offers help rather than reminding them of a task:

> *"Hey \[name\] — saw you started the Olera application. Anything I can help with? Takes about 10 minutes
> and there are agencies near campus hiring right now."*

**Do not automate this in 2.0.** The manual nudge is also the fastest way to learn *why* students stall — and
that reason is worth more right now than the saved minutes.

## 4. Vetting

The application already collects everything providers have historically asked for. Logan, on why: *"there's
not a home care provider that's going to want any more information or less information than this."* It
matured through repeated provider feedback, so **do not add fields casually** — but do keep listening.

Your vetting job is completeness and plausibility, not judgment:

| Check | Looking for |
|---|---|
| Complete profile | No blank required sections |
| Availability | Real, specific hours — not "flexible" |
| Contact info | A working email and phone |
| Plausibility | An actual student, actually near this campus |
| Red flags | Anything a provider would want to know before an interview |

**Do not gatekeep on quality.** Providers decide who they want to interview. Our job is completeness and
honesty; theirs is selection.

**When a provider asks for something new** — a certification, a test, a specific availability — log it and
tell Chantel. If two providers ask for the same thing, it belongs in the application. That is exactly how
the application got good in the first place.

## 5. Keeping candidates warm

Students go cold fast — they are applying to many things at once, and a job that goes quiet is a job they
forget.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| On activation | *"You're live. Agencies near \[University\] can now see your profile."* |
| Weekly, while unmatched | A short note: how many agencies are active, what is coming |
| On an interview invite | **Same-day nudge to accept.** This is the moment that decays fastest |
| After an interview | *"How did it go?"* — within 1 day, both sides |
| On an offer | Call them. Do not rely on email for the decision moment |
| No activity in 3 weeks | Check in; ask whether they are still looking |

**Watch for cash anxiety at the offer.** The build plan flags the student fee as *"the one seam where the
student could balk"* (see **E-7** and **C1**). If a student hesitates at the money moment, **that is a
finding — log it**, do not just push.

## 6. Seeding a new site

The chicken-and-egg problem is real, and it has one honest answer: **start supply before you need it.**

| Week | Target |
|---|---|
| 1 | Partners contacted; flyer distributed |
| 2 | First applications arriving |
| 3 | **8+ live candidates — the minimum viable board** |
| 4 | 15+ live candidates (**Gate C**) |

**Do not let a Client log in to an empty board.** If demand is running ahead of supply, hold the conversion
meetings (per `../01-EXECUTION-PLAYBOOK.md §1`) — do not slow prospecting, and do not put a converted
provider in front of nothing.
