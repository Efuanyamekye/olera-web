MedJobs Sales Lead Operations
Sales Lead view of the MedJobs 2.0 operating system · 4 September 2026

A role view of the MedJobs 2.0 Master Implementation Matrix. Every instruction here comes from that document, which stays the source of truth. Nothing has been added to the operating model; where the master leaves a question open, it is marked GAP / DECISION NEEDED rather than answered here.

ROLE PURPOSE Hold the two meetings the Admin Team books, and turn each into something the Consumer Relations Manager can act on: a converted provider on one side, an agreed channel plan on the other. The Sales Lead owns a narrow, decisive middle of both pipelines.

WHAT YOU OWN PR2 provider meeting held. ST2 advisor meeting held. Logging both outcomes. Naming the handoff in the room. Where a physician in the room changes the answer during ST3 to ST7, you share ownership with the Consumer Relations Manager.

WHAT YOU DO NOT OWN Prospecting, outreach and booking, which are the Admin Team's (PR1, PR-OUT, ST1, ST-OUT). Client success, university activation, matching, fulfilment and billing, which are the Consumer Relations Manager's or the Portal's.

WHO HANDS WORK TO YOU The Admin Team, at the booked meeting, on both sides. The row arrives in the Meetings queue with its full timeline.

WHO YOU HAND WORK TO The Consumer Relations Manager, once the outcome is logged.

Receiving the Admin Team handoff

A booking is the handoff. The Admin Team books the 30-minute Student Caregiver Program slot, usually live on a call or off a reply that names a time, and the row lands in Meetings at olera.care/admin/medjobs/in-basket?tab=meetings. Once the time has passed it sits under NEEDS LOGGING, with the instruction on the tab itself: "log the outcome to move the row forward."

Below NEEDS LOGGING, FINDING A TIME holds rows still coordinating by email. That is a holding state the Admin Team sets by hand, not a meeting, and not yours.

Read the row before the call. The timeline shows every email, reply and call that produced this meeting, including what they said when they replied. On the provider side the drawer's NEXT STEP reads "On the calendar" and the state chip reads "Replied, they replied, meeting booked."

Required actions

  1. Read the row before the call. The timeline shows every email, reply and call that produced this meeting, including what they said when they replied.
  2. Run the standard structure and close with the soft agreement ask: no rush to sign, we just need it before you interview your first student.
  3. Log the outcome the same day. A meeting held and unlogged is a row that stops moving.
  4. Write down what was promised in the notes. A note on its own logs the meeting.
  5. Rebook a no-show once, warmly. The option opens Calendly for you; people miss meetings.
  6. A no-show that cannot be rebooked goes back into outreach. It is not a decline. The row should re-enter a call-and-email sequence rather than sit in Meetings or close.

ST2 Advisor meeting held

Objective Hold the meeting, agree which of the five activation channels are open to us, and hand the Consumer Relations Manager a plan they can act on without asking again.

This meeting is not a sale. It is a channel-planning session. The provider meeting ends in a commercial agreement; this one ends in a route to students. The advisor already believes pre-health students want caregiving hours. What we do not know is how their campus reaches them, and the output is the ST3 to ST7 plan.
Trigger The booking lands the row in Meetings, under NEEDS LOGGING once the time has passed
Tools Meetings tab · the partner row and its timeline · Log meeting outcome, the same modal and the same three outcomes as the provider side · video for the meeting
Communications Booking confirmation and reminder from Calendly · the post-meeting note with the ready-to-send copy and flyer the advisor asked for · the results-back note that carries the next ask
Completion criteria Outcome logged, the agreed channels and their named contacts written down, and the relationship handed to the Consumer Relations Manager
Handoff Sales Lead to Consumer Relations Manager. Interested / went well launches activation and hands over the channel plan; the other two close the row

Required actions

  1. Read the row before the call. Advisors notice when you already know what they told the last person.
  2. Open by naming what students get, not what we want: hours, a letter, and experience that strengthens a med, PA or nursing application. That is the advisor's job, which makes it common ground.
  3. Then work the five channels in order. This is the body of the meeting.
  4. Ask for their read, not just their permission. "Which of these actually reaches pre-health students here?" An advisor telling you the job board is dead and the listserv is everything saves the Consumer Relations Manager a month.
  5. Get a name for every open channel. A channel with no named contact is not a channel.
  6. Ask what they need from us to make it easy, being copy, a flyer, a date, then send it within two days.
  7. Log the outcome the same day, with the channel plan in the notes.

The five channels

Channel Ask The answer the Consumer Relations Manager needs
ST3 University job board Is there a board pre-health students actually check? Who posts, and does it need approval? The board, the posting route, and who approves
ST4 Student organisations Which orgs reach these students, pre-med, pre-nursing, AMSA chapters? Who runs them this term? Org names and current officers, with a warm introduction where they will make one
ST5 Campus events Which fairs or events are worth attending, and what are the deadlines and costs? Event, date, registration deadline, who books the table
ST6 Advisor listservs Do you email these students directly? Would you send something for us? Which list, who sends it, how often they are willing, and the copy they want
ST7 Professors and class visits Which professors teach the courses these students take? Would an introduction be welcome? Named professors, and explicitly whether we may approach them

Permission is the deliverable on ST6 and ST7. A listserv send and a class visit both happen on someone else's authority. Write down who granted it and what exactly they agreed to, because the Consumer Relations Manager will be acting on it weeks later without having been in the room.

When the advisor skips the meeting

Some advisors will not take a call. They reply to the outreach by simply telling us what to do: post it here, email this officer, send me copy for the listserv. Take the advice and act on it. A channel the advisor handed us is worth more than the meeting that would have produced it. Two things still hold.

  1. Capture it exactly as if it came from a meeting, being channel, named contact and permission, so the Consumer Relations Manager inherits the same plan either way.
  2. Keep pushing for the meeting, gently. Not to unlock the channels; those are already running. A named advisor is what makes the second term easier than the first, and the moment to ask is when you report results back: here is what your listserv produced. Can we talk about the spring?

What must be recorded

Booked time, attendee, outcome, the channel plan and permissions as notes, and any named contacts offered.

The handoff to the Consumer Relations Manager

The boundary is the logged outcome. Before it, the row is yours. After it, the relationship is the Consumer Relations Manager's, on both sides.

Name the handoff in the room. On the provider side the master makes this an explicit step: "our consumer relations team will follow up with you on next steps." The provider leaves expecting the next contact, and expecting it from someone else. A warm introduction to a named function beats a cold email from a stranger a week later.

Provider side University side
What passes over The converted provider, the meeting notes, and anything promised The channel plan: which channels were agreed, the named contact for each, and the permission granted
How it passes The provider drawer timeline, which the Consumer Relations Manager reads before making contact As prose in the meeting notes
What the outcome does Interested / went well launches activation and moves the relationship on Interested / went well launches activation and hands over the channel plan
What happens next The Consumer Relations Manager sends the terms email within one business day of the meeting The Consumer Relations Manager activates every agreed channel within two weeks of the meeting

Two things about this handoff are worth stating plainly, because both are in the master and both bear on whether a relationship becomes ownerless.

Nothing tells the Consumer Relations Manager a provider is waiting. Logging the meeting outcome should alert them; it does not. See B4 under gaps.

The channel plan is the handoff, and it travels as prose. The master says this is "enough to work from and not enough to report on." See B6 under gaps.

Gaps and decisions needed

Each of these is a place the master document leaves open or names as unbuilt. None is resolved here.

GAP / DECISION NEEDED · "The standard structure" of the provider meeting is not written down. PR2 instructs the Sales Lead to "run the standard structure" and gives only its closing move, the soft agreement ask. The master specifies the ST2 meeting in full, channel by channel, but nowhere sets out the provider meeting's agenda. Either it exists outside this document and should be brought in, or it needs writing. Not resolved here.

B2 · A no-show re-enters outreach automatically. A missed meeting is not a decline. The outcome opens Calendly to rebook, and nothing catches the row if the rebook never happens.

B4 · An alert when a meeting converts, putting the client in front of the Consumer Relations Manager with the meeting notes attached. Not built. Nothing tells them a provider is waiting, which is why naming the handoff in the room matters as much as it does.

B6 · Structured channel capture at the meeting: which channels were agreed, the named contact for each, and the permission granted, as fields rather than prose. The plan lives in the meeting notes. Workable for one operator, unreportable across campuses.

B7 · A state for channels agreed, no meeting yet, carrying an open ask for a relationship meeting. No such state exists. The row either sits in Meetings without a meeting or looks like one that never engaged. This is the state an advisor who skipped the meeting leaves behind.

B8 · A partner-facing booking event. Advisors book the provider's event. The invite copy carries over and reads acceptably to an advising office; the slug home-care-agency-manager-interview in the URL does not.

B1 · The Meetings tab holds only booked meetings. Finding a time is a hand-set state inside Meetings, which muddies the queue you work from.

B12 · Channel attribution. Which of the five channels produced a student. Channels are not modelled as distinct entities, so the plan you agree in ST2 cannot yet be judged by results.
