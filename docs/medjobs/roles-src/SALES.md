# MedJobs Sales Lead Operations

A role view of the MedJobs 2.0 Master Implementation Matrix. Every instruction here
comes from that document, which stays the source of truth. Nothing has been added to
the operating model; where the master leaves a question open, it is marked
**GAP / DECISION NEEDED** rather than answered here.

**ROLE PURPOSE** Hold the two meetings the Admin Team books, and turn each into
something the Consumer Relations Manager can act on: a converted provider on one side, an
agreed channel plan on the other. The Sales Lead owns a narrow, decisive middle of
both pipelines.

**WHAT YOU OWN** PR2 provider meeting held. ST2 advisor meeting held. Logging both
outcomes. Naming the handoff in the room. Where a physician in the room changes the
answer during ST3 to ST7, you share ownership with the Consumer Relations Manager.

**WHAT YOU DO NOT OWN** Prospecting, outreach and booking, which are the Admin Team's
(PR1, PR-OUT, ST1, ST-OUT). Client success, university activation, matching,
fulfilment and billing, which are the Consumer Relations Manager's or the Portal's.

**WHO HANDS WORK TO YOU** The Admin Team, at the booked meeting, on both sides. The
row arrives in the **Meetings** queue with its full timeline.

**WHO YOU HAND WORK TO** The Consumer Relations Manager, once the outcome is logged.

## Receiving the Admin Team handoff {: #receiving .nobreak }

A booking is the handoff. The Admin Team books the 30-minute *Student Caregiver
Program* slot, usually live on a call or off a reply that names a time, and the row
lands in **Meetings** at `olera.care/admin/medjobs/in-basket?tab=meetings`. Once the
time has passed it sits under **NEEDS LOGGING**, with the instruction on the tab
itself: *"log the outcome to move the row forward."*

Below **NEEDS LOGGING**, **FINDING A TIME** holds rows still coordinating by email.
That is a holding state the Admin Team sets by hand, not a meeting, and not yours.

**Exhibit M. Meetings tab.** What the handoff looks like on your side. **NEEDS LOGGING** carries meetings whose time has passed, over **FINDING A TIME**, which is the Admin Team still coordinating and not yours.

![Exhibit M. Meetings tab](../matrix-src/exhibits/M-meetings-tab.png)

**Read the row before the call.** The timeline shows every email, reply and call that
produced this meeting, including what they said when they replied. On the provider
side the drawer's **NEXT STEP** reads *"On the calendar"* and the state chip reads
*"Replied, they replied, meeting booked."*

## PR2 Provider meeting held {: #pr2 }

**Objective** Hold the meeting, convert the provider, and capture what was promised.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | The booking lands the row in **Meetings**, under **NEEDS LOGGING** once the time has passed |
| **Tools** | Meetings tab &#183; the provider drawer and its timeline &#183; **Log meeting outcome** modal &#183; Calendly for a rebook |
| **Communications** | Booking confirmation and reminder from Calendly &#183; the post-meeting details email with the agreement &#183; a polite closing note when the answer is no |
| **Completion criteria** | Outcome logged and the relationship handed to the Consumer Relations Manager |
| **Handoff** | **Sales Lead to Consumer Relations Manager.** *Interested / went well* launches activation and moves the relationship on; the other two close the row |

### Required actions

1. **Read the row before the call.** The timeline shows every email, reply and call
   that produced this meeting, including what they said when they replied.
2. **Run the standard structure** and close with the soft agreement ask: *no rush to
   sign, we just need it before you interview your first student.*
3. **Log the outcome the same day.** A meeting held and unlogged is a row that stops
   moving.
4. **Write down what was promised** in the notes. A note on its own logs the meeting.
5. **Rebook a no-show once, warmly.** The option opens Calendly for you; people miss
   meetings.
6. **A no-show that cannot be rebooked goes back into outreach.** It is not a decline.
   The row should re-enter a call-and-email sequence rather than sit in Meetings or
   close.

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Interested / went well** | Logs the meeting and launches the activation sequence |
| **No-show / reschedule** | Logs a no-show and opens Calendly to rebook |
| **Not interested** | Sends a polite closing note and stops outreach |

**Exhibit N. Log meeting outcome.** A **BOOKED** panel confirming the calendar entry and the attendee, the three outcomes with their consequences, and a notes field. Behind it the drawer shows **NEXT STEP** over a timeline running back through every reply and send.

![Exhibit N. Log meeting outcome](../matrix-src/exhibits/N-log-meeting.png)

### What must be recorded

Booked time, attendee name and address, outcome, notes and commitments.

## ST2 Advisor meeting held {: #st2 }

**Objective** Hold the meeting, agree which of the five activation channels are open
to us, and hand the Consumer Relations Manager a plan they can act on without asking again.

**This meeting is not a sale. It is a channel-planning session.** The provider meeting
ends in a commercial agreement; this one ends in a route to students. The advisor
already believes pre-health students want caregiving hours. What we do not know is how
their campus reaches them, and the output is the ST3 to ST7 plan.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | The booking lands the row in **Meetings**, under **NEEDS LOGGING** once the time has passed |
| **Tools** | Meetings tab &#183; the partner row and its timeline &#183; **Log meeting outcome**, the same modal and the same three outcomes as the provider side &#183; video for the meeting |
| **Communications** | Booking confirmation and reminder from Calendly &#183; the post-meeting note with the ready-to-send copy and flyer the advisor asked for &#183; the results-back note that carries the next ask |
| **Completion criteria** | Outcome logged, the agreed channels and their named contacts written down, and the relationship handed to the Consumer Relations Manager |
| **Handoff** | **Sales Lead to Consumer Relations Manager.** *Interested / went well* launches activation and hands over the channel plan; the other two close the row |

### Required actions

1. **Read the row before the call.** Advisors notice when you already know what they
   told the last person.
2. **Open by naming what students get**, not what we want: hours, a letter, and
   experience that strengthens a med, PA or nursing application. That is the advisor's
   job, which makes it common ground.
3. **Then work the five channels in order.** This is the body of the meeting.
4. **Ask for their read, not just their permission.** *"Which of these actually
   reaches pre-health students here?"* An advisor telling you the job board is dead
   and the listserv is everything saves the Consumer Relations Manager a month.
5. **Get a name for every open channel.** A channel with no named contact is not a
   channel.
6. **Ask what they need from us to make it easy**, being copy, a flyer, a date, then
   send it within two days.
7. **Log the outcome the same day**, with the channel plan in the notes.

**Exhibit L. The booking page.** What both a provider and an advising office see: **Student Caregiver Program**, 30 minutes, your name, and the pitch, over a date and time picker. One event serves both funnels, which is what **B8** is about.

![Exhibit L. The booking page](../matrix-src/exhibits/L-calendly-booking.png)

### The five channels

| | Channel | Ask | The answer the Consumer Relations Manager needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ST3** | University job board | Is there a board pre-health students actually check? Who posts, and does it need approval? | The board, the posting route, and who approves |
| **ST4** | Student organisations | Which orgs reach these students, pre-med, pre-nursing, AMSA chapters? Who runs them this term? | Org names and current officers, with a warm introduction where they will make one |
| **ST5** | Campus events | Which fairs or events are worth attending, and what are the deadlines and costs? | Event, date, registration deadline, who books the table |
| **ST6** | Advisor listservs | Do you email these students directly? Would you send something for us? | Which list, who sends it, how often they are willing, and the copy they want |
| **ST7** | Professors and class visits | Which professors teach the courses these students take? Would an introduction be welcome? | Named professors, and explicitly whether we may approach them |

**Permission is the deliverable on ST6 and ST7.** A listserv send and a class visit
both happen on someone else's authority. Write down who granted it and what exactly
they agreed to, because the Consumer Relations Manager will be acting on it weeks later
without having been in the room.

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Interested / went well** | Logs the meeting and launches the activation sequence |
| **No-show / reschedule** | Logs a no-show and opens Calendly to rebook |
| **Not interested** | Sends a polite closing note and stops outreach |

### When the advisor skips the meeting

Some advisors will not take a call. They reply to the outreach by simply telling us
what to do: post it here, email this officer, send me copy for the listserv. **Take
the advice and act on it.** A channel the advisor handed us is worth more than the
meeting that would have produced it. Two things still hold.

1. **Capture it exactly as if it came from a meeting**, being channel, named contact
   and permission, so the Consumer Relations Manager inherits the same plan either way.
2. **Keep pushing for the meeting, gently.** Not to unlock the channels; those are
   already running. A named advisor is what makes the second term easier than the
   first, and the moment to ask is when you report results back: *here is what your
   listserv produced. Can we talk about the spring?*

### What must be recorded

Booked time, attendee, outcome, the channel plan and permissions as notes, and any
named contacts offered.

## Where the Sales Lead stays involved after the handoff {: #after }

Three places, and only three. The master gives the Sales Lead no other role after the
meeting is logged.

| Where | What the master says |
|---|---|
| **ST3 to ST7 ownership** | The stage is owned by the *"Consumer Relations Manager, with the Sales Lead where a physician in the room changes the answer"* |
| **ST5 campus events** | Who acts: *"Consumer Relations Manager and Sales Lead attend"* |
| **ST7 professors and class visits** | Who acts: *"Consumer Relations Manager and Sales Lead, once permitted"* |

Read the second and third together with the first: the Consumer Relations Manager works the
plan, and you are in the room where a physician's presence is what makes the ask land.

## The handoff to the Consumer Relations Manager {: #handoff }

The boundary is the logged outcome. Before it, the row is yours. After it, the
relationship is the Consumer Relations Manager's, on both sides.

**Name the handoff in the room.** On the provider side the master makes this an
explicit step: *"our consumer relations team will follow up with you on next steps."* The
provider leaves expecting the next contact, and expecting it from someone else. A warm
introduction to a named function beats a cold email from a stranger a week later.

| | Provider side | University side |
|---|---|---|
| **What passes over** | The converted provider, the meeting notes, and anything promised | The channel plan: which channels were agreed, the named contact for each, and the permission granted |
| **How it passes** | The provider drawer timeline, which the Consumer Relations Manager reads before making contact | As prose in the meeting notes |
| **What the outcome does** | *Interested / went well* launches activation and moves the relationship on | *Interested / went well* launches activation and hands over the channel plan |
| **What happens next** | The Consumer Relations Manager sends the terms email within one business day of the meeting | The Consumer Relations Manager activates every agreed channel within two weeks of the meeting |

Two things about this handoff are worth stating plainly, because both are in the
master and both bear on whether a relationship becomes ownerless.

**Nothing tells the Consumer Relations Manager a provider is waiting.** Logging the meeting
outcome should alert them; it does not. See **B4** under gaps.

**The channel plan is the handoff, and it travels as prose.** The master says this is
*"enough to work from and not enough to report on."* See **B6** under gaps.

## Exceptions and escalation {: #exceptions }

Only the exceptions the master document defines are listed here.

| Situation | What the master says to do |
|---|---|
| **A no-show** | Rebook once, warmly. The outcome option opens Calendly for you |
| **A no-show that cannot be rebooked** | It is not a decline. The row should re-enter a call-and-email sequence rather than sit in Meetings or close. Nothing catches the row today if the rebook never happens, so this is a hand-worked exception |
| **An advisor who will not take a meeting** | Take the advice they give in the reply and act on it. Capture channel, named contact and permission exactly as if it came from a meeting, and keep pushing gently for the meeting when you report results back |
| **A meeting held and not yet logged** | Log it the same day. A meeting held and unlogged is a row that stops moving |

## Gaps and decisions needed {: #gaps }

Each of these is a place the master document leaves open or names as unbuilt. None is
resolved here.

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**GAP / DECISION NEEDED &#183; "The standard structure" of the provider meeting is not
written down.** PR2 instructs the Sales Lead to *"run the standard structure"* and
gives only its closing move, the soft agreement ask. The master specifies the ST2
meeting in full, channel by channel, but nowhere sets out the provider meeting's
agenda. Either it exists outside this document and should be brought in, or it needs
writing. Not resolved here.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B2 &#183; A no-show re-enters outreach automatically.** A missed meeting is not a
decline. The outcome opens Calendly to rebook, and nothing catches the row if the
rebook never happens.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B4 &#183; An alert when a meeting converts**, putting the client in front of the
Consumer Relations Manager with the meeting notes attached. Not built. Nothing tells them a
provider is waiting, which is why naming the handoff in the room matters as much as it
does.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B6 &#183; Structured channel capture at the meeting**: which channels were agreed,
the named contact for each, and the permission granted, as fields rather than prose.
The plan lives in the meeting notes. Workable for one operator, unreportable across
campuses.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B7 &#183; A state for _channels agreed, no meeting yet_**, carrying an open ask for a
relationship meeting. No such state exists. The row either sits in Meetings without a
meeting or looks like one that never engaged. This is the state an advisor who skipped
the meeting leaves behind.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B8 &#183; A partner-facing booking event.** Advisors book the provider's event. The
invite copy carries over and reads acceptably to an advising office; the slug
`home-care-agency-manager-interview` in the URL does not.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B1 &#183; The Meetings tab holds only booked meetings.** *Finding a time* is a
hand-set state inside Meetings, which muddies the queue you work from.
</div>

<div class="gap" markdown="1">
**B12 &#183; Channel attribution.** Which of the five channels produced a student.
Channels are not modelled as distinct entities, so the plan you agree in ST2 cannot yet
be judged by results.
</div>
